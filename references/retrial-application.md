# Retrial Application Profile

Use this profile when drafting a 再审申请书 challenging unfavorable parts of an effective civil judgment. See [style-handoff.md](style-handoff.md) for team-specific prose.

## 1. Procedural function

The application should enable the retrial court to understand the case quickly and see, at first reading, a small number of specific errors that fit a retrial ground and materially produced the unfavorable result.

It is not an expanded appeal. It must identify the error in the effective adjudication, not merely repeat why the applicant's original position was preferable.

## 2. Material boundary

Ordinarily visible:

- first- and second-instance pleadings, supplied evidence, evidence opinions, and hearing records;
- first- and second-instance judgments;
- the applicant's prior confirmed positions;
- current application-stage materials available by the cutoff.

Exclude later retrial decisions, later responses, supervision materials, and any later lawyer final reserved for evaluation.

## 3. Use the mandatory orientation module

Unless the lawyer expressly directs otherwise at the outline gate, begin the substantive part with a compact account of:

- the material case background and legal relationship;
- the first- and second-instance procedural history;
- the real dispute focus;
- the material holdings and adjudicated results;
- the point at which the effective judgment changed or rejected the applicant's position.

This module is not a full chronology. Include only what the retrial judge needs to understand the later error analysis. It should orient, not consume the document.

## 4. Reconstruct the judgment's decisive chain

For each material unfavorable holding, identify:

1. the court's conclusion;
2. the factual finding, evidence treatment, rule, or procedure supporting it;
3. the inference connecting that premise to the conclusion;
4. the disposition affected;
5. the available record that supports or undermines the chain;
6. the possible statutory retrial ground.

Do not use broad labels such as “事实认定错误” until the specific failed link has been identified.

## 5. Select only decisive errors

Rank candidate errors by:

- ability to change the result;
- certainty and page-verifiability of the supporting record;
- fit with an available retrial ground;
- logical simplicity and visibility to a new judge;
- consistency with the lawyer's prior positions;
- absence of a stronger counterexplanation.

The outline should ask: if only one or two errors could remain, which would most likely justify reopening or changing the result? Do not give every arguable error equal status.

Reserve or delete:

- harmless defects;
- disagreements with discretionary wording that do not affect the disposition;
- issues abandoned or contradicted by the applicant's earlier position without an approved explanation;
- weak errors included only to appear comprehensive;
- new theories or authorities not approved by the lawyer;
- facts unavailable by the procedural cutoff.

## 6. Build each error as a closed chain

A selected section should generally establish:

```text
the exact judgment error
→ the judgment passage and page
→ the verified fact, evidence, rule, or procedure the judgment missed or misused
→ why the judgment's inference cannot stand
→ how the error changed the disposition
→ the applicable retrial ground
```

The heading should reveal the error itself. Prefer formulations that state what the judgment confused, omitted, assumed, or applied incorrectly. Avoid headings that only announce a legal category.

When attacking evidence evaluation, distinguish failure to review evidence, misstatement of content, improper allocation of burden, and an unsustainable inference. They are not interchangeable.

## 7. Use force through precision

Attack the reasoning, not the court. Strong language is justified by a demonstrated contradiction, omitted decisive fact, impossible inference, or clear procedural consequence. State the judgment passage fairly before explaining the defect.

Alternative reasoning can be useful when it shows that the judgment fails under either of two plausible characterizations. Keep the alternatives mutually clear and tied to the requested result.

## 8. Research and statutory fit

Use the law already present in the record and lawyer-confirmed theory first. If a selected error requires new statutory, case, or doctrinal research, identify the exact research question in the analysis report and obtain approval before adding it.

Do not assume an article number or version from memory. Verify the law applicable to the relevant time and procedural request before final text.

## 9. Requests and basic information

Separate the requested retrial disposition from the grounds. Do not invent a target retrial case number. Confirm the target court, effective judgment identifiers, original procedural roles, requested relief, and signature information before Word production.

## 10. Outline gate questions

Before drafting, ask the lawyer to confirm:

- the orientation module's scope and length;
- the small number of decisive errors selected from the complete error map;
- the statutory retrial ground for each selected error;
- the requested disposition and any alternative request;
- permitted new research, evidence, or facts;
- issues expressly reserved or deleted;
- target length and order of attack.
