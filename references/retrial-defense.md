# Retrial Defense Profile

Use this profile when drafting a 再审答辩状 for a party preserving favorable parts of an effective judgment. See [style-handoff.md](style-handoff.md) for team-specific prose.

## 1. Procedural function

The document must answer two linked questions:

1. why the applicant's underlying factual, evidentiary, legal, or procedural proposition is wrong or does not change the result;
2. why that proposition does not satisfy the asserted retrial ground.

A retrial defense is not a second-instance defense with renamed parties. It addresses the effective judgments and the statutory threshold for reopening them.

## 2. Material boundary

Ordinarily visible:

- first- and second-instance pleadings, supplied evidence, evidence opinions, and hearing records;
- first- and second-instance judgments;
- the retrial application and its supplied attachments;
- current retrial notices and lawyer-confirmed materials formed by the cutoff.

Exclude later retrial rulings, judgments, supervision materials, and any later lawyer final reserved as an evaluation answer.

## 3. Separate the retrial label from the underlying dispute

For every asserted retrial ground, extract:

- the cited statutory or procedural label as written by the applicant;
- the underlying factual or legal proposition;
- whether the proposition is truly new or an old dispute newly packaged;
- how the first- and second-instance judgments treated it;
- what prior lawyer position and evidence remain available;
- whether the proposition, even if assumed, could change the effective result.

Do not answer only the statutory label. Do not answer only the old entity-level dispute. Connect both.

## 4. Select and group issues

The analysis report must account for every material asserted retrial ground. The outline may merge several grounds when they rely on the same fact, evidence chain, or alleged omission.

Usually include:

- each ground that could satisfy a retrial threshold or change the disposition;
- alleged omissions, procedural violations, evidence errors, or new evidence with a plausible result impact;
- a material change in the applicant's characterization of the same claim, loss, obligation, or evidence;
- favorable reasoning from the effective judgments that directly answers the application;
- subject-specific defenses for each represented respondent.

Usually reserve or delete:

- first-instance defenses not raised as retrial grounds and unnecessary to the effective result;
- broad claims that the judgments are correct without analyzing the application;
- repeated quotations from both judgments when one concise holding is enough;
- arguments directed only to another respondent;
- collateral disputes that do not affect whether retrial should be initiated.

## 5. Organize each selected section

A useful section pattern is:

```text
asserted retrial ground and underlying proposition
→ whether the point is new, repeated, or changed
→ how the effective judgments addressed it
→ record-based response
→ why it does not satisfy the retrial ground or alter the result
```

The heading should state the substantive defect and the retrial consequence, not merely repeat a statutory article number.

Use favorable judgment language economically. Explain why the holding remains supported; do not substitute quotation for reasoning.

## 6. Address changed positions and procedural history

When the applicant changes the label or consequence of an earlier claim:

- verify the earlier pleading or hearing statement at the original page;
- distinguish a clarification from a material change;
- state the effect of the change without overstating it as a binding admission;
- show why the new packaging does or does not fit the asserted retrial ground.

When a claimed omission was actually resolved on another ground, preserve that distinction. Court silence, rejection, and other-ground resolution are not interchangeable.

When the application alleges an omitted claim, compare three records separately: requested relief in the operative pleading and any recorded amendment, explanations made in argument or evidence review, and the findings and disposition of the effective judgments. An unchanged amount may support different theories. A new characterization in the reasons does not establish that a separate claim was presented below. If only the judgment's account of a party's statement is available, attribute it to that judgment and mark the original filing or transcript for review.

## 7. Keep respondents separate

In a multi-respondent matter, mark every retrial proposition as shared, respondent-specific, or irrelevant to the represented subject. A joint background does not require each respondent to answer relief directed solely at another.

## 8. Tone and compression

The stance is restrained and finality-focused. Use “不属于所主张的再审事由”, “原审已经审查”, “系对原有争议的重新表述”, or equivalent conclusions only after demonstrating the record basis.

Do not turn the defense into a third full trial brief. Concentrate on why the application fails to cross the retrial threshold and why the effective result remains supported.

## 9. Outline gate questions

Before drafting, ask the lawyer to confirm:

- which asserted grounds should be merged by underlying issue;
- whether any new evidence or procedural allegation requires separate treatment;
- which effective-judgment passages may be quoted;
- respondent-specific divisions;
- alternative grounds worth preserving;
- target length and any issue to reserve or delete.
