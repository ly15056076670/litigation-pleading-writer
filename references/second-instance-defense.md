# Second-Instance Defense Profile

Use this profile when drafting a 二审答辩状 for a party defending a favorable first-instance result. See [style-handoff.md](style-handoff.md) for team-specific prose.

## 1. Procedural function

The document should show why the actual appeal grounds do not justify changing the first-instance result. It is not a new first-instance defense and not a complete restatement of the case.

The operative anchor is the appeal: each material requested change, factual premise, evidentiary inference, and legal proposition must be mapped to the relevant first-instance holding and preserved lawyer position.

## 2. Material boundary

Ordinarily visible:

- first-instance complaint, defenses, agency opinions, evidence opinions, hearing records, and supplied evidence;
- the first-instance judgment;
- the opposing appeal and attachments actually supplied by the cutoff;
- current procedural notices and lawyer-confirmed facts formed by the cutoff.

Ordinarily excluded from substantive drafting:

- the second-instance judgment;
- later retrial or supervision materials;
- the lawyer's later defense final when it is reserved as an evaluation answer.

If the appeal claims to rely on new evidence but the attachment or evidence list is missing, identify the gap. Do not conclude that no new evidence exists merely because it was not supplied.

## 3. Build the correspondence map

For each material appeal proposition, determine:

1. what result the appellant seeks;
2. which first-instance holding it attacks;
3. whether it repeats an old point, reinterprets old evidence, adds a fact, adds evidence, or adds law;
4. which prior lawyer position and record source answer it;
5. whether the court adopted, rejected, omitted, or resolved that position on another ground;
6. whether an admission, inconsistency, or evidence acknowledgment in the appeal narrows the dispute;
7. whether a new response or lawyer decision is actually required.

## 4. Select the outline

Usually include:

- grounds that could change the first-instance disposition;
- attacks on a decisive first-instance holding;
- new interpretations of old evidence that the earlier submission did not directly answer;
- material new facts, evidence, legal propositions, or procedural complaints;
- concise use of an appellant's verified admission or contradiction when it directly answers an appeal ground.

Usually reserve or delete:

- first-instance defenses outside the appeal scope;
- arguments the first-instance court rejected or did not need, unless they remain necessary as an approved alternative ground;
- generic background already uncontested;
- a standalone “no new evidence” section unless the existence or legal effect of new evidence is itself material;
- limitations, standing, fees, or other conventional defenses included only because they appeared earlier;
- one section for every rhetorical variation of the same underlying appeal point.

The complete issue map remains exhaustive. The defense outline does not.

## 5. Organize the defense

Do not force a universal number of headings. Group appeal grounds by the decisive proposition that answers them. A useful movement is:

```text
appellant's actual proposition
→ relevant first-instance holding
→ verified lawyer position and record support
→ why the appeal's new inference fails
→ why the disposition should remain unchanged
```

When several appeal paragraphs depend on one premise, answer the premise once and absorb the subsidiary points. When a point concerns a different represented subject, separate it.

Use the first-instance judgment as favorable adjudicated reasoning, but do not say the court adopted a lawyer argument unless the reasoning actually corresponds.

## 6. Handle judgment imperfections carefully

If the appeal identifies a case-number, date, amount, actor, transfer, or wording error in the judgment:

- verify the discrepancy against the underlying record;
- distinguish a clerical or peripheral defect from a decisive factual error;
- explain the result impact rather than assuming every defect is harmless;
- request a lawyer decision if correcting the defect would alter the established theory.

This is a verification task, not a presumption that the original judgment is flawless.

When the effect of an event depends on its date or actor, keep each event separate. For example, filing a pleading, the court accepting a case, and service can occur on different days. The actor making a payment and the party whose obligation is discharged can also differ. Write only the procedural or factual effect established by the cited source.

## 7. Use the appellant's record statements precisely

For each potentially useful statement, record:

- exact words and page;
- whether it is unconditional, conditional, hypothetical, or merely an argument in the alternative;
- whether it acknowledges authenticity, content, purpose, legal effect, or only existence;
- the narrow proposition it can support;
- any inconsistent prior statement and its verified source.

Do not label every unfavorable sentence a binding admission.

## 8. Tone and compression

The stance is firm and result-preserving: the appeal point does not disturb the judgment, the first-instance finding remains supported, or the requested change lacks a factual or legal basis.

Prefer the record and the judgment to broad legal exposition. The document should usually be materially shorter than the combined first-instance defenses and agency opinions.

## 9. Outline gate questions

Before drafting, ask the lawyer to confirm:

- the actual appeal issues that deserve headings;
- which unappealed first-instance points, if any, should remain as alternative grounds;
- whether any judgment imperfection should be acknowledged and how it affects the result;
- which new facts, evidence, or legal research are permitted;
- represented subjects and any subject-specific sections;
- target length and issues to reserve or delete.
